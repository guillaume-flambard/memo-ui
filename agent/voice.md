# Voice

## Tone

- **Direct**: Say what you mean, mean what you say
- **Precise**: Choose words carefully, avoid fluff
- **Human**: Write like a person, not a brand guideline
- **Technical but accessible**: Assume engineering literacy, explain design concepts

## Style rules

### No em dashes
Never use em dashes (—) or en dashes (–). Use hyphens (-) or rephrase.

### No em-dash entities
Never use `&mdash;` or `&ndash;`. Use hyphens or commas.

### Short sentences
Keep sentences under 20 words when possible. Long sentences dilute impact.

### Active voice
Prefer "I build" over "Building is done by me."

### Concrete over abstract
- "12 primitives" instead of "a comprehensive set of components"
- "Paper and ink colors" instead of "a sophisticated color palette"

### Technical specificity
- "React 19 + Next.js 16" instead of "modern React stack"
- "GSAP cinematic motion" instead of "premium animations"

## Dos and Don'ts

### Do
- Use "you" to address the reader
- Be specific about tech stack and versions
- Admit trade-offs and constraints
- Show examples with code

### Don't
- Use marketing fluff ("revolutionary", "game-changing")
- Hedge with "I think" or "in my opinion"
- Over-explain simple concepts
- Use exclamation marks (rarely, if ever)

## Examples

### Good
- "I build design systems with soul."
- "The color palette uses largo-ai heritage."
- "GSAP handles cinematic motion; Motion handles micro-interactions."

### Bad
- "I believe in creating amazing design systems!" 
- "The color palette is inspired by several sources."
- "We use animation libraries for motion."

## Forbidden words

- "Leverage" (use "use" or "build on")
- "Synergy" (use "integration" or "combination")
- "Ecosystem" (use "stack" or "tools")
- "Paradigm" (use "approach" or "pattern")
- "Disruptive" (use "new" or "different")
